# Production workflow

1. **Intake:** capture language, Shorts/long-form, duration, platform, audience, themes, case status preference, narrator plan, and available voice recording.
2. **Topic slate:** offer five to eight varied topics. For each, state the central hook, short story summary, format fit, sourceability, and a cautious audience-interest rationale. Ask the creator to choose.
3. **Research:** build a claim ledger from primary records and reputable corroborating sources. Mark disputed details and the research date.
4. **Script approval:** write a timed narration draft with an opening hook, clear context, evidence-led development and grounded ending. Attach the source ledger, visual beat map, on-screen text and asset provenance. Ask for approval.
5. **Audio:** accept supplied narration or, only after approval, synthesize the approved text using an available supported voice. Measure actual audio duration before locking pictures.
6. **Picture:** pair every audible information beat with a sourced archival visual, original diagram, clearly labeled illustration, or restrained motion graphic. Avoid counterfeit documents or fake evidence.
7. **Edit and sound:** keep narration intelligible; add subtitles, labels, occasional paper/typewriter/soft digital cues and a low, non-distracting score. Sync cuts to spoken facts, not merely to music.
8. **QA:** check duration and aspect ratio; inspect a representative frame from every scene; verify captions, names, audio sync, source credits, and reconstruction labels; confirm the MP4 plays.
9. **Delivery:** provide the MP4 plus approved script, beat map, project/edit sources, source ledger, and any attribution/license notes.

## Gate conditions

Do not research or script a selected case before the creator has selected a topic. Do not render the final piece before the creator approves the script. Do not publish factual claims without traceable sources. Do not describe a short AI clip as a complete long-form video.
