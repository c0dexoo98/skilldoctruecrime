# Ethics and fact-check checklist

## Before choosing a story

- Is the topic supported by reliable, accessible sources?
- Is the case closed, unresolved, or actively litigated? Label its status accurately.
- Would this framing add avoidable harm to survivors, family members, or private individuals?
- Does the pitch rely on a sensational claim that cannot be substantiated?

## During research

Maintain a claim ledger with these fields: claim, exact scope, source URL, publisher/author, publication date, source type, supporting passage or record location, and confidence/status. Prefer primary records for dates, court outcomes and agency statements. Corroborate consequential claims with independent, reputable reporting where possible.

Classify each statement as established, attributed, disputed, or unknown. Attribute allegations. Record source conflicts rather than silently choosing the more dramatic version. Distinguish a charge from a conviction and an exoneration from a procedural reversal. Recheck the latest status of any active case immediately before publication.

## In the script and edit

- Never invent dialogue, quotes, motives, forensic findings, timelines or investigative actions.
- Do not imply guilt when it has not been established by a court or reliable record.
- Do not use generated documents, faces, maps, or scenes as if they were authentic evidence. Label illustrative reconstructions on screen and in the description.
- Avoid graphic detail, victim-blaming and unnecessary disclosure of private addresses or personal information.
- Treat social-media posts and anonymous claims as leads, not proof.
- Include concise source credits in the end card or description; retain a fuller internal source ledger.
- Use licensed, public-domain or permission-cleared imagery and music; preserve license and attribution notes.

## Final review

A second pass should verify names, dates, locations, case status, quotes, subtitles, attributions, image provenance, privacy, narration intelligibility, and that the final title/thumbnail do not overstate the evidence.
